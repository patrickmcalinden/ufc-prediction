# Methodology

This site predicts the outcome of upcoming UFC fights using an XGBoost
classifier trained on every UFC fight since ESPN's records become
reliable. Every prediction shown was locked in *before* the fight took
place — the Performance page is the public scoreboard.

## Data

All raw data comes from ESPN's MMA section:

- **Fighter profiles** — name, weight class, record, physical stats, stance
- **Fights** — both fighters, weight class, title status, winner, method, round
- **Per-fight stats** — strikes by location and stance (distance / clinch /
  ground), takedowns, submissions, advances, knockdowns

Data lives in a local Postgres database. Each event is scraped once
before the card to lock predictions, and again after results are
available. The scraper handles re-runs idempotently — fight results
overwrite previous nulls but a recorded winner is never wiped by a
later scrape.

## Models

Three models run side by side. Each gets its own tab on the Performance
page and its own locked record.

| Model | Inputs | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **v1** | Elo ratings + title flag (7 features) | Baseline |
| **v2** | v1 + historical striking and grappling stats (22 features) | Known data leak (below) |
| **v3** | Tuned Elo + fight history (25 features) | Current |

### v3 features

Everything v3 sees comes from the full fight schedule (every UFC and
Contender Series bout ESPN lists) plus fighter dates of birth, computed
using only fights *before* the one being predicted:

- *Tuned Elo* — K = 64, a 50% bigger swing for KO/TKO/submission wins,
  and ratings drift back toward average after a year out of the cage
- *Experience* — prior fights
- *Win rate* and *finish rate* (share of wins by KO/TKO/sub)
- *KO/TKO losses* — a rough proxy for durability
- *Layoff* — days since the last fight
- *Streak* and *last-3 form*
- *Age*

Each appears for fighter A, fighter B and as the A − B difference. If a
value is unknown for either fighter it's blanked for both, so "missing
data" can never itself become a signal.

### v2's data leak

v2 uses per-fight striking and grappling stats. Those are only scraped
for fighters who appear on a recent card, so historically, *having* stats
meant *still being active today* — and fighters who are still active are
the ones who kept winning. In training fights where only one fighter had
stats, that fighter won 75.6% of the time. The model learned the
coverage pattern rather than the fighting, which is why its backtest
looked great and its live record didn't. v2 keeps running so its record
stays public, with a warning on its tab.

### Mirroring

To prevent positional bias the training set is mirrored: each fight
appears twice, once as A vs. B and once as B vs. A with the label
flipped.

## Evaluation

Every model is scored with a **walk-forward backtest**: for each year
from 2021 on, train on every earlier fight and predict that year's
fights. That's how the model is actually used — trained on the past,
predicting the next card.

Before a model is saved, a **leak check** looks at every A/B feature
pair: among fights where only one fighter has a value, if "has a value"
alone predicts the winner, training fails. That check is what catches
v2.

The production model is fit on the full history before predicting an
upcoming event. Each prediction snapshot records the model version so
historical picks remain attributable after a retrain.

## What the model can't see

A short list of things known to affect outcomes that aren't in the
feature set:

- *Camp changes* — switching gyms / coaches mid-camp
- *Reported injuries or cuts*
- *Short-notice replacement fights*
- *Weight cuts that look brutal on fight week*
- *Style matchups beyond what aggregate stats capture*

In short: the model is a baseline that knows what's quantifiable.
Expert handicappers reliably beat baselines like this on a small
fraction of fights where they have private information. The honest
goal here is to be a solid 55–60% prior, calibrated, with all results
public.

## Honesty

The Performance page only counts **locked pre-event snapshots** —
predictions recorded before the fight happened, not retroactive
backtests. The schema enforces this: locked rows can't be retroactively
modified, and the unique constraint on `(fight_id, model_version)`
prevents accidental double-counting after retrains.
