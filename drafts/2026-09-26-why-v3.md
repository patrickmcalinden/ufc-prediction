# The problem

On paper, **v2** was the best thing I'd built. It added striking and grappling history on top of Elo, and cross-validation put it at **60.9%**, well clear of v1's 55%.

Live, it went the other way. After 151 locked picks, v1 was at **61.6%** and v2 at **57.6%**. Over the most recent 56 picks, v2 was at 51.8% — basically a coin flip.

A model that's great in testing and worse in reality is almost always telling you the test is broken.

# The leak

v2's striking and grappling stats come from `fighter_stats`, and I only scrape those for fighters who show up on an upcoming card. That sounds harmless, but think about what it means historically:

- About 91% of fighters whose last fight was in 2026 have stats.
- For fighters who stopped before 2021, it's 0–5%.

So in the training data, *having stats at all* really meant *still fighting today*. And fighters who are still fighting years later are, on average, the ones who kept winning.

The model found this immediately. In training fights where only one fighter had stats history, **that fighter won 75.6% of the time**. v2's single most important feature was striking accuracy, and most of what it encoded was "has this fighter been scraped?"

At prediction time, both fighters are on an upcoming card, so both have been scraped. The signal vanishes, and v2 falls back to roughly Elo-level performance. The cross-validation couldn't catch it because the test folds had exactly the same bias.

When I scored v2 only on fights where the leak can't help (both or neither fighter has stats), it lands at **55.3%**.

# What changed

**The evaluation.** Every model now gets a *walk-forward backtest*: for each year from 2021, train on everything before it and predict that year. That's exactly how the model is used for real.

**A leak check.** Before a model is saved, training looks at every "fighter A / fighter B" feature pair. If "only one fighter has this value" predicts the winner, training fails. v2 fails it. Had this existed a few months ago, v2 would never have shipped.

**v3.** A new model built only from data with full coverage — the complete fight schedule, which includes every UFC and Contender Series bout regardless of how anyone's career went:

- A retuned Elo (bigger K, extra credit for finishes, ratings drift back after a year out)
- Experience, win rate, finish rate, KO losses
- Layoff, current streak, last-three form
- Age

If a value is missing for either fighter, it's blanked for both, so "missing data" can't become a signal again.

# The numbers

Walk-forward backtest, 2021–2026 (3,221 fights):

| Model | Accuracy | Log loss |
|---|---|---|
| v1 | 56.4% | 0.680 |
| v2 (leak-free fights) | 55.3% | 0.686 |
| **v3** | **61.3%** | **0.655** |

v3's recent years are stronger still (about 65% in 2025–26), mostly because the database starts in 2005, so fighter histories are more complete the later you go.

# What happens to v1 and v2

Nothing gets deleted. Every pick either model locked was a real pre-fight prediction, and the record is how I found this in the first place. All three models run side by side for the next few cards, v2's tab carries a warning, and once v3 has a live record of its own, the older two get retired — still visible, no longer picking.

The backtest says v3 is better. The live record gets the final say.
